# Application Layer

The Application Layer is responsible for realizing the domain-specific processes and business rules associated with REDD+ projects and establishing the interface between the Blockchain infrastructure and external systems. Its architectural responsibilities arise from the requirements associated with project documentation, MRV evidence, carbon credit life-cycle management, governance, socio-environmental safeguards, data management, and integration with external registries and accounting systems.

To address these requirements, the Application Layer comprises seven logical functional components:

- **Project and Methodology Management (A1):** responsible for managing project documentation, including the PDD, project boundaries, emission baselines, applicable methodologies, and supporting evidence associated with additionality assessments. This component supports the identification and versioning of the documentation and methodological conditions applicable to project-related decisions.

- **MRV Evidence Management (A2):** responsible for receiving, organizing, and managing information associated with MRV processes. This responsibility includes maintaining the provenance of supporting evidence, identifying its sources and responsible entities, associating evidence with the applicable methodology, and recording validation and verification decisions. This component supports the traceability of external assessments without assuming that their environmental validity can be established exclusively through computational mechanisms.

- **Carbon Credit Management (A3):** responsible for managing the identification, representation, and life-cycle states of carbon credits. Its responsibilities encompass issuance, transfer, retirement, and, where applicable, cancellation and invalidation. This component also supports the recording of reversal-related events and their association with the affected credits or accounting records. Credit-related operations must comply with the applicable state transition conditions and authorization requirements.

- **Governance and Business Rule Execution (A4):** responsible for implementing the domain-specific rules governing project-related operations, stakeholder roles, authorization policies, and institutional requirements. This component supports the enforcement of the applicable operational conditions, including those associated with credit issuance, transfer, and retirement. It also supports the recording and management of applicable socio-environmental commitments, including stakeholder consultations, consent-related documentation, benefit-sharing arrangements, and grievance resolution processes.

- **Data and Audit Management (A5):** responsible for managing the storage, retrieval, versioning, and auditability of project-related information. This component supports the association between on-chain records and off-chain information, the preservation of historical records, and the traceability of authorized corrections. The selection of storage mechanisms must consider information sensitivity, availability, retention requirements, and the applicable data protection policies.

- **External System Integration (A6):** responsible for supporting communication with external systems, including MRV platforms, carbon registries, government information systems, carbon markets, and other relevant data sources. This component supports the exchange of information through defined interfaces, the identification of external sources, and the handling of communication and data exchange failures.

- **Registry and Accounting Reconciliation (A7):** responsible for supporting the reconciliation of credit-related information with applicable external registries and accounting systems. This responsibility includes associating internal and external identifiers, identifying discrepancies in credit status and accounting records, and supporting the procedures required to address inconsistencies. Where applicable, this component also supports the recording and reconciliation of information associated with jurisdictional REDD+ programs, authorization requirements, and corresponding adjustments.

These components represent logical architectural responsibilities rather than mandatory software modules. Depending on the selected technological configuration, their realization may involve smart contracts, conventional application services, external information systems, or combinations of these mechanisms.

The functional components of the Application Layer contribute to the requirements identified in Table 4 of the accompanying manuscript. Project and Methodology Management (A1) supports the traceability of project documentation and methodologies (TC1). MRV Evidence Management (A2) supports the traceability of MRV evidence and associated validation and verification decisions (TC2). Carbon Credit Management (A3) supports credit life-cycle management, reversal-related accounting, and the prevention of duplicate issuance and repeated credit use within the system (TC3, TC4, and TC5).

Governance and Business Rule Execution (A4) supports the enforcement of credit-related operational conditions, stakeholder authorization, information access policies, and socio-environmental commitments (TC3, TC4, TC5, GP1, GP2, and GP3). Data and Audit Management (A5) supports documentation versioning, evidence traceability, information protection, the preservation of socio-environmental records, and the auditability of recorded information (TC1, TC2, GP2, GP3, and CI2). External System Integration (A6) supports the exchange of information with external MRV systems and registries (TC2 and IT1). Registry and Accounting Reconciliation (A7) supports reversal-related accounting and reconciliation with external registries and jurisdictional accounting systems (TC4 and IT2). Together, these components also contribute to the preservation of architectural responsibilities across compatible technological configurations (IT3).

The execution of domain-specific business rules constitutes a technological decision point in Arq-REDD+. Smart contracts represent one possible implementation mechanism, allowing certain operations to be executed automatically when the specified conditions are satisfied. However, not all domain-specific processes must be executed on-chain. Depending on their computational characteristics, confidentiality requirements, and dependence on external information, some processes may be implemented through off-chain application services or external systems.

The representation of carbon credits constitutes another technological decision point. Credits may be represented using individual identifiers, identifiable batches, tokenization mechanisms, or other compatible data representations. Regardless of the selected representation, the implementation must preserve the identification, traceability, and valid life-cycle transitions of the corresponding credits. Tokenization alone does not establish the environmental validity or legal recognition of a carbon credit.

Data management also constitutes a technological decision point. Information may be stored directly on the distributed ledger, maintained in external repositories, or distributed across both environments. When information is maintained off-chain, references, identifiers, cryptographic commitments, or other compatible mechanisms may be used to associate it with the corresponding ledger records. Such mechanisms may contribute to verifying the integrity of referenced information but do not independently guarantee its availability, authenticity, or factual correctness.

Integration with external systems constitutes a further technological decision point. APIs, oracles, messaging services, or other compatible interoperability mechanisms may be employed according to the characteristics of the systems involved. The selection of these mechanisms must consider source identification, authentication, information provenance, data validation, and the handling of unavailable, inconsistent, or corrected external information.

The reconciliation of credit-related information with external registries requires particular attention. The consistency of records maintained within a Blockchain system does not independently guarantee consistency with external registries or prevent double counting across different systems. Consequently, the Application Layer must support the identification and reconciliation of relevant external records and the handling of detected discrepancies according to the applicable governance and accounting procedures.

The responsibilities of the Application Layer depend on coordinated interactions with the Network and Consensus layers. Stakeholder authentication and information protection mechanisms must support the authorization and confidentiality policies defined by the application. Likewise, the execution of credit-related operations must be compatible with the transaction validation, ordering, state management, and finality properties provided by the Consensus Layer.

The architectural invariants of the Application Layer concern the preservation of its domain-specific functional responsibilities and interface contracts. The technologies employed to execute business rules, represent carbon credits, manage data, and integrate external systems constitute configurable implementation decisions. Their selection must satisfy the applicable domain requirements and compatibility constraints without assuming that all technological alternatives provide equivalent functionality, security, or performance.

Although the Application Layer supports the computational realization of REDD+ processes, it does not replace the institutional, environmental, or regulatory procedures upon which the legitimacy of these processes depends. In particular, the environmental integrity of carbon credits remains dependent on the applicable methodologies, supporting evidence, independent assessments, and institutional governance.
